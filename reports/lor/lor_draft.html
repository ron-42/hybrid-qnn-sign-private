To Whom It May Concern

Subject: Letter of Recommendation for Ronak Bhalgami

It is my pleasure to recommend Ronak Bhalgami, who completed the B.Tech in Computer Engineering (2021–2025) at the U & P U Patel Department of Computer Engineering, CSPIT, CHARUSAT, for graduate study and research opportunities. As Head of the Department, I have known Ronak since joining our department in 2021 and have followed both academic progress and research work closely.

Ronak graduated with a CGPA of 9.42 out of 10, ranking in the top 1% of the batch, and earned a perfect 10.00 SGPA in both the second and eighth semesters. Such consistency across four years reflects not only ability but steady discipline.

Ronak's research interests lie in deep learning and quantum machine learning, and the record is already substantial for this stage. With fellow co-authors, Ronak published the research chapter “Enhancing Deep Fake Image Generation and Detection” in the Springer Nature book Artificial Intelligence for Next Generation Computing, and presented it at the AICTA 2024 conference. Ronak has also authored an arXiv preprint, “Pixels for Programs? A Cross-Provider Case Study,” which evaluates image and text representations of source code across leading vision-language models.

Ronak's most recent work, carried out with fellow co-authors, is titled “Compact Quantum Channel Attention for Static ASL Recognition: A Controlled Study with Parameter-Matched Baselines.” It places an eight-qubit variational quantum circuit inside a DenseNet121 network as a channel-attention gate for American Sign Language recognition. The gate uses 17,464 parameters, about 7.5 times fewer than a standard squeeze-and-excitation (SE) block. The paper is currently under peer review, and the team will soon present the work at a relevant international conference.

What impressed me most is the scientific discipline behind this work. Near-perfect accuracy on the standard benchmark split said little about the quantum component, so Ronak rebuilt the evaluation around harder questions. The study compares the quantum gate against no attention, SE attention, and a classical network matched to within 16 parameters. It adds low-data experiments at 1%, 5% and 10% of the training set across three seeds, and tests generalisation on the MU HandImages dataset by holding out one of five signers at a time, with paired statistical tests. The quantum gate reached 94.50% mean accuracy on unseen signers, above SE at 93.72%, yet no difference between models was statistically significant. Ronak reported this candidly rather than overstating it, a maturity that is rare at this stage and essential to good research.

Across more than fifty controlled training runs on limited hardware, Ronak showed strong command of PyTorch, PennyLane, transfer learning with DenseNet121 and ResNet50, reproducible experiment pipelines, and statistical analysis. Ronak also takes feedback seriously: when advised that the backbone should not be frozen, the main experiment was redesigned and re-run accordingly.

During the degree, Ronak was an active member of the university's AI/ML Club and DSA Club and took part in their workshops, reflecting both technical breadth and a genuine interest in learning alongside peers.

Ronak also brings practical engineering maturity. After an AI/ML internship at Mindarray Systems (Motadata) during the final year, Ronak joined the company as an Associate AI/ML Engineer and is now an AI Engineer at Middleware.io (YC W23), building production multi-agent systems, while continuing independent research on quantum kernel methods against classical baselines.

Ronak is curious, self-driven and rigorous, and combines strong fundamentals with honest scientific judgement. I am confident that Ronak will thrive in a demanding research environment, and I recommend Ronak Bhalgami wholeheartedly for graduate study and research. Please feel free to contact me for any further information.
